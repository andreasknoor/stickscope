# Research dossier: ELEVATOR (3D figure)

Researched 2026-11-23. Figure: model descends nearly vertically, stalled, nose-high, with almost no forward speed. Wording is mode-independent (up/down elevator, left/right aileron, rudder left/right). All statements are paraphrases; quotes are 10 words or fewer.

Method note: pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises pages. Sources I could not read (RCUniverse forum threads return HTTP 403, Google Sites needs login, the baronerosso PDF is compressed) are NOT cited. German term: no readable source uses "Fahrstuhl"; German sources (RCLine, de.wikipedia) use "Elevator". The German term "Fahrstuhl" is therefore NOT CONFIRMED.

## 3 Phase outline (CONFIRMED statements only)

1. Entry: model is slowed to the stall point with enough height (S2, S5). Elevator is pulled up progressively (S3). Throttle is cut or reduced (S2).
2. Transition: elevator reaches full / nearly full up (S1). The model settles into a steep to near-vertical descent (S8).
3. Hold: elevator stays full up (S1). A little throttle is added and adjusted to keep the nose up (S6). Rudder corrects heading (S11). Small brief aileron corrections catch wing rock (S12, S17).
4. Exit: release / reduce the up elevator and add throttle, fly out (S21). Option: continue into a harrier (S24).

Not in the confirmed outline: entering into the wind (S4), any minimum height, the throttle level in the hold if idle is meant (S7), and which control leads for wing rock (S13).

## 5 Conflicts and open points

Conflicts
- C1 (S7) Throttle during the hold. F (Spillmann/McConville): idle, model falls vertically like a stone. A, B, C, D, E: some throttle added to hold the nose up or fuselage level. Possible reconciliation (not evidence): different model types or different hold variants. Not resolvable from sources.
- C2 (S13) Wing levelling. A: rudder is the main tool, aileron small and brief, with rudder alongside. B: usually no aileron; over-correcting drops a wing. D, E, F: ailerons control wing rock / bank. Not resolvable from sources.
- C3 (S9 vs S10) Descent angle: D gives 60 degrees or steeper, C gives about 45 degrees to nearly backwards depending on wind. May be different flavours; recorded as two single-source numbers.
- C4 (S29) B says a lower dive entry is better, while C and F say start high. Different entry context; no resolution.

Open points
- Minimum safe height for the exit: no readable source gives a figure for the elevator.
- Entry speed: no airspeed number in any source.
- Model / setup dependence (wing loading, CG, power): not found in readable sources. Only wind (S10), propwash/P-factor (S20), airframe quality (S18) and flap mix (S19) appear, each single-source.
- Max exit rate / how much throttle: only "add throttle", no amount.
- Source F should be re-read on the original page; the English original by McConville was not located.
- Independence of E (blog, undated) from A is unverified.
- German term "Fahrstuhl" not found in any readable source.

## 6 Difficulty / category remarks

- Beginner-level 3D: A "first 3D maneuver many pilots learn"; B "Very Easy"; D "most basic 3-D maneuver" (2001). Three groups agree on entry-level 3D.
- No source rates it as advanced or pro. Source E rates the hover as one of the hardest (not the elevator).
- No source states a prerequisite level beyond "3D" itself; E gives practice altitude advice only.
- Suggested category for the app: beginner 3D / first 3D figure (supported by S30). Do not label it "beginner in general" (non-3D) without further evidence.
