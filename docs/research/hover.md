## 3 Phase outline (CONFIRMED statements only)

1. **Setup and practice basics.** Large control throws (S22a), simulator first (S18), safe height and short holds (S17a). No numbers confirmed.
2. **Entry.** Fly a harrier, keep adding up elevator until vertical (S7). Throttle: NOT confirmed beyond "short bursts after corrections" (S8). The flight direction at entry and which side faces the pilot are NOT confirmed.
3. **Hanging in the hover.**
   - Throttle holds altitude (S1); short bursts, no sustained high throttle (S8).
   - Aileron opposes the torque rotation, direction against the prop rotation (S2, S3); right aileron in the models described (S4, model-dependent).
   - Rudder and elevator hold the model vertical (S5) with small, short, early corrections (S6). Direction of elevator corrections: NOT GIVEN.
   - Belly toward the pilot: rudder stick toward the dropping wing (S9).
4. **Exit.** NOT GIVEN by any confirmed statement. (R2 mentions throttling up to bail out; single source, not confirmed.)

## 5 Conflicts and open points

- **S17b Starting height.** R1: at least 150 ft (source-given), slow climb allowed. R2: start about 15 ft and move closer to the ground. R4 (PW): pull up at about 10 m. Not reconcilable from the text. R2's 15 ft may mean something else (the page summary was ambiguous). Do not show a number.
- **S22b Throws.** R3: 35-45 deg 3D rate. R4: 40 deg elevator is "not much". Do not show a number.
- **S24 Aileron-elevator mix.** R5: Werner (about 20 percent raised ailerons helps some models), peterbach (depends on model), Badflyer (mix helps entry, not torque) vs Martin Greiner (pointless, train instead). Optional setup, not for the basic figure.
- **S27 Terminology.** "Hovern" in R4 first means two figures (sinking with full up elevator vs. hanging on the prop); thread resolves to the second. German pilots may also use "Fahrstuhl/Elevator" for the first figure. Use "Hovern/Schweben" only for hanging on the prop.
- **S13 vs S14 (tension, not logged as conflict).** R1: left rudder during the pitch-up; R8 (summary only): constant right rudder in the hover. Might be different phases, but no source says so. Keep both unpublished until a second source for each appears.
- **Prop rotation to aileron direction.** No source states which rotation direction needs right aileron. Do not show a fixed direction as fact; show "against the prop torque, right aileron in the examples".
- **Unread leads (NOT evidence; found only in search summaries of unreadable pages):** RCU posts say elevator and rudder senses flip between canopy-in and belly-in; a nose-up of about 80-85 deg is easier to learn; most models need roughly 1/2 to 3/4 right aileron (this one matches R1); up elevator with right rudder to keep vertical. A Flite Test video (VoroRC) and RCU threads should be read in a browser if possible.
- No German-language source describes elevator/rudder sense or entry in detail; the DE sources (R3, R4, R5) cover aileron and throttle only. Fabian Günther's 3D page (fabian-guenther.ch) did not load.
- Which side of the model faces the pilot at entry, exit technique, and which elevator direction corrects a nose tilt are the main gaps for the choreography.

## 6 Difficulty / category remarks

- Advanced. R1 rates it among the hardest figures of its guide, "Master-level", with four continuously modulated inputs (source-given). R8: "can be very challenging". R3: hard at the start because the control roles change. R4/R5: practice is the only way. R2: do not rush; fundamentals first.
- Prerequisite: harrier (R1, R2). Hover comes before torque roll (S20).
- Beginner suitability: no source calls it beginner-friendly. Suggested StickScope category: advanced (not pro), based on S19, since R1 ranks "Master" within an intro guide to 3D.
