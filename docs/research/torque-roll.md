Access date for all sources: 2026-11-23. Wording is mode-independent (left/right aileron, up/down elevator, rudder left/right). Paraphrases only.

Important limitation: the evidence base is thin. Several key pages (RCU forums, RCGroups, modelflying.co.uk, Flite Test body text, MAN members pages) were not readable (HTTP 403/402, or video-only). Nothing from unread pages is used as evidence. No German-language instruction text covering reversal, exit or throttle detail was found; German evidence comes only from forum threads.

## 3 Phase outline (CONFIRMED statements only)

1. Prerequisite: stable hover first (S20); practise in calm air (S14), with altitude margin (S21), ideally after simulator time (S22).
2. Hover entry: pull to fully vertical, the prop carries the model (S24, S1).
3. Starting the rotation: do not counter the torque; the roll develops by itself in a clean hover (S6, S2). Direction: left (S3; viewpoint unstated).
4. During rotation: rudder and elevator keep the model's long axis vertical (S7). Right aileron (against the torque) slows or stops the rotation (S5). Throttle in small steps to hold altitude (S10).
5. Exit: NOT CONFIRMED (no source readable). Only S5 (opposite aileron stops rotation) is confirmed, which is a mechanism and not an exit recipe.

## 4 Hypotheses

(a) Torque rotates the model, left for a clockwise prop seen from behind: PARTLY CONFIRMED. Torque as cause confirmed (S2). Rotation to the left confirmed (S3). R1 ties left to a counterclockwise prop without naming the viewpoint; the claim "clockwise seen from behind" is NOT FOUND in any source as stated. Model dependence: only implied (S3 flagged model-dependent by the author's logic; no source states it explicitly for direction).
(b) Rotation speed metered with aileron: CONFIRMED in substance (S5, S6, S15, S25): aileron against the torque slows or stops rotation; releasing it lets it turn. "Metered" proportionally is not stated; R9 gives hover/harrier values only.
(c) Nose kept vertical with rudder and elevator: CONFIRMED (S7).
(d) Reversal when the belly faces the pilot: only SINGLE-SOURCE (S8, R1). Related difficulty statements in R4 and R5 are consistent but not explicit. Not confirmed.
(e) Throttle controls altitude and is increased slightly during the roll: first half CONFIRMED (S10). "Increased slightly during the roll" NOT FOUND; sources only say hold altitude with small changes, and S11/S13 are single-source and different.
(f) Exit with opposite aileron then full throttle climb: NOT FOUND (opposite aileron to stop rotation is mechanism-level S5 only; the full-throttle climb is not in any readable source).
(g) Expert/pro figure: PARTLY. Sources say it is harder than the hover (R1, R5) or very hard (R7), with one dissent (R6). No source uses the labels beginner/advanced/pro; R9 calls the hover "master-level" and calls it a prerequisite.

## 5 Conflicts and open points

- C1 (S23): R1 and R5 say torque roll is harder than hover; R6 (Stephan S.) says hover was harder for him and torquing is easier; another R6 poster says the reverse. Unresolved.
- C2: "left" direction (S3) vs viewpoint. R1 links left to a counterclockwise prop (viewpoint unstated); R4 says left as power increases with no prop direction. Mapping to propeller direction as seen from behind is open and must not be inferred.
- C3 (S11 vs R5): R4 says power adds left torque; R5 Hansjuerg says aileron adds drag so more throttle is needed and more rotation follows. Different mechanisms, not a direct contradiction.
- Open: exit/stop procedure; entry into the roll from a flying hover (aileron offset versus release); reversal detail (single source); rotation-speed values; throttle trend during the roll; model dependence of direction. Unread leads (not evidence): RCU "how to torque roll", RCGroups BoneDoc clinic, Flite Test video, MAN member article "Advanced aerobatics: the torque roll".
- Several German statements come from 2001-2002 forum posts with glow-engine context (mixture, stalling), which may not transfer to electric models.

## 6 Difficulty / category remarks

- Hard or very hard: R1 (harder than hover; many learn hover quickly but need much longer for torque rolls), R5 (Hansjuerg: looks easier than hover, actually much harder; Oli: hours or days; Klaus: about four weeks with a Super-Diablotin), R7 (very hard; a top competitor flew a clean one only once, per vazifar).
- Dissent: R6 Stephan S. found hover harder.
- Hover is the prerequisite (R9 calls hover "master-level"; R8 calls hover the most basic 3D manoeuvre and the roll an extension).
- Wind makes it much harder (R6 Jan, R2/R3).
- Category: evidence supports "advanced" (after hover). "Pro/expert" is not stated by any source (R3 calls a well-done one the interviewee's favourite and most impressive). Beginner is contradicted.
