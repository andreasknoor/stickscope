# Research dossier: Flat spin (RC 3D, upright power-on; variants noted)

Access date for all sources: 2026-11-23. Rule applied: a statement is CONFIRMED only if at least two independence groups agree. Own knowledge is not used as evidence. Statements are paraphrases. Controls use mode-independent wording. Variant is upright unless marked (INV = inverted, KE = via knife-edge spin).

Overall finding: the evidence base is thin. Several of the best sources (RCUniverse, RCGroups) were blocked and could not be read. Most readable sources are forum threads, and only one is a magazine-style how-to article (FlyRC). No source gives stick-travel percentages, degrees for the spin itself, durations or heights in metres.

## 3 Phase outline (CONFIRMED statements only)

1. Preparation (S13, S14, S15, S22, S26, S27, S28): model with aft CG and large throws, enough height, idle that holds. Practise in a simulator and master conventional spins first.
2. Entry (S1, S2, S3): bleed speed to stall, elevator full up, rudder full into the spin direction. Aileron at entry is not confirmed (S4 conflict).
3. Spin established (S5): hold elevator and rudder.
4. Flatten (S6, S7, S8, S11, S12): aileron gradually toward neutral or to the opposite side, throttle added (about half where numbers given). Too much or too little aileron can flip the model out (S11).
5. Exit (S16, S17, S18): release/centre sticks; if rotation continues, opposite rudder; several more turns are possible.
6. Recovery (S19, S22): nose is down, regain airspeed, then level out; extra height needed. Throttle during exit is in conflict (S21).

## 4 Conflicts and open points

- S4 aileron at entry. R1 and R3: same direction as rudder (R1 explicitly for the conventional spin). R5 wov (upright funflyer): left rudder with right aileron, i.e. opposite. R4 Grenda: depends on spin sense and model. Cannot be reduced to one rule.
- S6 flattening: "toward neutral" (R1) versus "to the opposite side" (R2, R4, R5). Likely the same idea (move from entry aileron through neutral toward opposite), but not stated that way by any source.
- S21 throttle in exit: idle (R1) versus power (R4 Matthias R, Rudi T; R1 also says power speeds recovery).
- S24 and S25 recoverability and CG effect: R7, R4 Rudi T, slider say aft CG/flat spin may be unrecoverable; R4 loopeng and Grenda disagree.
- S34 throttle effect on spin: faster/climbing (Grenda) versus worse descent (flyforfun, KE variant).
- Independence caveat: R1 and R2 have similar wording; R4 and R5 posters share threads.
- NOT FOUND: any stick-travel percentage for flat spin phases; spin rate (turns per second) and how to control it other than throttle/aileron tuning; recommended height in metres or feet; typical number of turns to hold; rotation-direction preference of the model (seen only in blocked RCUniverse snippets); wind effects; electric-model specifics; pirouetting flat spin; a dedicated instructor or club guide in German; confirmation of the "common mistakes" beyond S10, S11, S28, S29.

## 5 Difficulty and category remarks

- R2: fairly complex, for advanced pilots; recommends simulator first. R1: not especially hard but needs finesse; requires master of conventional spins; planes vary greatly. R8 (G-WP): not necessarily a 3D manoeuvre by most definitions. R9: spin (upright and inverted) sits in Phase 5, "Advanced Aerobatics"; flat spin itself is only in the sport index. R7: flat spin equals loss of the model (opposite stance).
- Evidence supports "advanced" (R2, R9 indirect, R1 prerequisite), not beginner. Pro/expert label: NOT GIVEN by any source. Risk of unrecoverable spin is model-dependent (S23, S24).
